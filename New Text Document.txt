## Brand & Style

The design system is engineered for the high-stakes environment of enterprise healthcare. It prioritizes "Clinical Calm"—a visual philosophy that reduces cognitive load for practitioners while instilling a sense of stability and precision for patients.

The aesthetic follows a **Corporate / Modern** style with a focus on high-functional minimalism. By utilizing generous whitespace and a restricted, high-contrast palette, the system ensures that critical medical data remains the focal point. The interface feels intentional, quiet, and profoundly reliable, moving away from "tech-heavy" aesthetics toward a more human-centric, organized medical environment.

## Colors

The color strategy for this design system is rooted in traditional medical trust. The **Primary Blue** is used purposefully for core actions and navigation, while the **Soft Off-white** background prevents screen glare during long shifts.

Semantic colors (Success, Warning, Danger) are slightly muted to remain professional and avoid causing unnecessary alarm, while maintaining high accessibility ratios. Grays are biased toward "Cool Slate" to maintain a sterile, clean feel without feeling cold. Use `surface` for cards and modals to create a clear "object" hierarchy against the `background`.

## Typography

This design system utilizes a dual-font pairing to balance character with utility. **Manrope** is used for headlines to provide a modern, refined tone. **Inter** is the workhorse for all body copy and UI elements, chosen for its exceptional legibility in dense data environments like patient charts and lab results.

For tabular data, use the `table-cell` style with **tabular numbers** enabled to ensure that columns of figures align vertically, making them easier to scan for medical professionals.

## Layout & Spacing

The layout philosophy follows a **Fluid Grid** model with strict 4px increments. Enterprise clinic software often requires high information density; however, this design system mandates "Generous Spacing" to prevent user error.

The standard layout uses a 12-column grid with 24px gutters. Sidebars should be fixed at 260px to provide a consistent anchor for navigation. Elements should utilize a "Comfortable" density by default (16px internal padding for cards), with a "Compact" mode available for data-heavy administrative tables (8px-12px padding).

## Elevation & Depth

This design system uses **Ambient Shadows** and **Tonal Layers** to define hierarchy. Unlike consumer apps, the elevation here is subtle to maintain a "flat" professional profile.

- **Level 0 (Background):** Soft Off-white (#F8FAFC).
- **Level 1 (Surface):** Pure White (#FFFFFF) with a 1px border (#E2E8F0). No shadow. Used for the main content canvas.
- **Level 2 (Cards):** Pure White (#FFFFFF) with a very soft shadow: `0px 1px 3px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.05), 0px 4px 6px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.02)`.
- **Level 3 (Modals/Popovers):** Used for focused tasks. Shadow: `0px 10px 15px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.05), 0px 4px 6px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.03)`.

Transitions between elevations should be instant or very fast (150ms) to feel responsive and utility-focused.

## Shapes

The shape language is disciplined and consistent. A **Soft** corner radius (8px/0.5rem) is applied to all primary containers, including cards, buttons, and input fields. 

Small components like checkboxes and tags use a smaller 4px radius, while "Pill" shapes are reserved exclusively for status indicators (chips) and search bars to distinguish them from actionable buttons.

## Components

### Buttons
Primary buttons use the Calm Medical Blue with white text. Hover states shift to a deeper navy-blue. Secondary buttons use a slate-gray outline or subtle light-gray fill to remain visually subordinate.

### Cards
Cards are the primary container for patient data. They must feature a 1px border (#E2E8F0) and the Level 2 shadow. Padding within cards should be a consistent 24px (lg spacing) to ensure readability.

### Form Inputs
Input fields use a 1px border with a subtle 2px inset shadow for a tactile feel. On focus, the border transitions to Primary Blue with a 3px soft glow (20% opacity). Labels are always positioned above the input in `label-md` style.

### Tables (Critical Component)
Tables are the heart of this design system. Use `table-cell` typography. Header rows have a light slate background (#F1F5F9) and bolded text. Row hovering should trigger a very subtle tint (#F8FAFC) to help eye-tracking across long rows of data.

### Status Chips
Status indicators for appointments (e.g., "Confirmed", "Cancelled") use the muted background versions of the semantic palette with high-contrast text to ensure they are glanceable but not distracting.

### Icons
Use **Lucide-style** outline icons with a 1.5px or 2px stroke width. Icons should always be monochrome (Slate-600) unless they are part of a destructive action (Red) or a primary call-to-action (Blue).
